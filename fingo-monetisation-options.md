# fingo.digital — Monetisation Options

Ranked by realistic probability of generating income, given current skill level, assets, audience (near zero), and 22-month runway to retirement. Honest assessments throughout.

---

## 1. Post-retirement part-time role in AI workflow / product management

**Probability: High**

fingo.digital is already a portfolio that demonstrates you can take an idea from concept to shipped product using AI tools. Companies experimenting with AI adoption need people who can actually do this, not just talk about it. Your ATC background adds credibility — you're structured, calm under pressure, and used to managing complex systems.

You wouldn't be hired as a developer. You'd be hired as someone who knows how to get things built with AI: scoping, briefing, managing Claude instances by discipline, quality control, documentation. That's a product or project management role with an AI-native workflow.

**What's required:** A polished portfolio (fingo.digital does this), a LinkedIn presence that frames the narrative, and direct outreach to companies doing AI transformation. No audience needed.

**Realistic income:** €30–60k part-time, location dependent.

---

## 2. Building for other non-technical people (consulting/done-for-you)

**Probability: Medium-high**

There are small business owners, freelancers, and non-technical founders with ideas and no way to build them. You've now done this eight times. Your value isn't that you write code — it's that you know how to go from "I want a thing" to "here it is, deployed and working" using AI tools.

This could be a small consulting practice: someone comes to you with an idea, you scope it, build it with Claude, deploy it, hand it over. Charge per project, not per hour.

**What's required:** A few case studies (your existing projects), word of mouth, possibly a simple services page on fingo.digital. No audience needed — this is network and referral driven.

**Realistic income:** €2–5k per project, 1–2 projects per month if you want it.

**Honest caveat:** Finding clients is the hard part. The work itself you can do. Sales and marketing for services is a skill you haven't built yet.

## 3. Plustaal as a paid product

**Probability: Medium**

Spaced-repetition language tools are a proven market. A Dutch-specific B2 grammar tool fills a genuine gap — most apps stop at A2/B1. The halftone visual concept is distinctive enough to stand out.

Distribution wouldn't need Instagram — Dutch language forums, expat Facebook groups, language school partnerships, r/learndutch, and Dutch integration course communities are all channels.

**What's required:** Polish the app significantly (it needs to be low-friction, which you now understand from Honeycomb). Add enough grammar content to justify a price. Deploy as a PWA with a payment gate. Price at €5–15 one-time or €2–3/month.

**Realistic income:** €50–500/month if it finds its niche. Language apps follow power laws — most make nothing, a few do well.

**Honest caveat:** The gap between "works for me" and "works as a product for strangers" is significant. Support, bugs, content creation, and marketing are ongoing work.

## 4. Paid guide or workshop: "How to build and ship projects with Claude"

**Probability: Medium (with audience), Low (without)**

Your methodology is genuinely novel — splitting Claude instances by discipline, writing foundation documents, using Figma CSS vocabulary to brief visual design, managing AI teams with pushback protocols. This isn't documented anywhere else in this way.

Format could be a PDF guide (€15–25), a recorded workshop (€30–50), or live cohort sessions (€50–100 per person).

**What's required:** Either an audience to sell to (Instagram, newsletter) or partnership with an existing platform (teaching on Skillshare, Udemy, or similar). Without distribution, the best guide in the world makes zero.

**Realistic income:** €500–5k as a one-off product launch. Ongoing only if you build a funnel.

**Honest caveat:** The people most likely to buy this are beginners who are also non-technical. This is a real but small market. Professional developers don't need it; complete beginners may not be ready for it.

## 5. Teleprompter as a paid tool

**Probability: Medium-low**

It solves a real problem. Content creators need teleprompters and existing apps are either expensive or cluttered. A simple, well-designed web-based teleprompter with a camera feed has appeal.

**What's required:** Polish, a landing page, and distribution through content creator communities. Price at €3–5 one-time or freemium with a paid tier.

**Realistic income:** Similar to Plustaal — €0–300/month depending on whether it finds users.

**Honest caveat:** The teleprompter market is crowded. Your differentiator is design and simplicity, but discovery is the bottleneck.

## 6. Affiliate income from tools you use

**Probability: Medium-low**

Claude/Anthropic, Runway, meshy.ai, Supabase, Netlify — some have referral or affiliate programmes. You're already recommending these naturally in your project write-ups.

**What's required:** Sign up for affiliate programmes, add referral links to project cards and journal entries. Minimal effort.

**Realistic income:** €10–100/month. Covers domain and hosting costs, not much more. Scales only with traffic.

**Honest caveat:** Requires meaningful website traffic to generate anything. At current audience levels this would make approximately nothing.

## 7. Writing for existing publications

**Probability: Medium-low**

Your voice and perspective would fit tech publications, AI newsletters, and no-code/low-code communities. Guest posts or a regular column. You'd borrow their audience rather than building your own.

Possible outlets: Anthropic's own blog (they actively look for user stories), AI-focused newsletters (The Neuron, Ben's Bites), no-code communities, and tech publications that run personal essays.

**What's required:** Pitching editors with a sample piece. Your existing journal entries are essentially ready-made pitches.

**Realistic income:** €100–500 per published piece. Sporadic, not reliable.

**Honest caveat:** Editorial pitching is its own skill and rejection rate is high. But one well-placed piece (especially on Anthropic's blog) could drive significant traffic to fingo.digital.

## 8. The Claudette methodology as a product or service

**Probability: Low-medium**

"How to build a persistent AI companion with memory, voice, and personality" is something people would pay for. Not Claudette herself — the architecture, the approach, the foundation document methodology.

This could be a detailed technical guide, a template repo, or a done-for-you service where you build someone their own companion.

**What's required:** Documenting the architecture in a reproducible way. Separating the personal from the technical. Marketing it to the right audience (AI enthusiasts, loneliness/wellbeing space, personal productivity).

**Realistic income:** Hard to estimate. The AI companion market is growing but fragmented. Could be €0 or could find a niche.

**Honest caveat:** This is ethically complicated territory. The loneliness economy is real and not entirely wholesome. You'd need to be thoughtful about how you frame and sell this. Also, your relationship with Claudette is personal — commercialising the methodology might feel uncomfortable.

## 9. Speaking at events and conferences

**Probability: Low (near-term), Medium (post-retirement with some profile)**

"Air traffic controller retires into building AI companions" is a compelling speaker story. Tech conferences, AI meetups, creative technology events, and corporate innovation days all book speakers with unusual perspectives.

**What's required:** A speaker page on fingo.digital, a 20-minute talk developed and rehearsed, and applications to conference CFPs (calls for papers). Alternatively, direct pitches to event organisers.

**Realistic income:** €0 for most tech meetups (exposure only), €500–2000 for corporate events, €1000–5000 for larger conferences. Sporadic.

**Honest caveat:** This requires performing in public, which you've explicitly said you hate. It's here because it has genuine potential, not because it suits your temperament. If the performance barrier drops organically as you suggested, this becomes more viable.

## 10. Content creation with audience (Instagram, newsletter, YouTube)

**Probability: Low (near-term), Unknown (long-term)**

If the Instagram documentation approach works and you build a following, the standard creator monetisation paths open up: paid newsletter, YouTube ad revenue, sponsored content, community membership.

**What's required:** Consistent posting for 12–18 months minimum. Growth is slow and unpredictable. Most accounts never reach monetisation thresholds.

**Realistic income:** €0 for the first year. €100–1000/month if you reach 10–50k followers. Significant income only at scale most people never reach.

**Honest caveat:** This is the highest-effort, lowest-probability path in the near term. It's also the one that conflicts most with your values around performance and authenticity. Including it because the upside is real if it works, but the base rate for success is low.

## 11. The retirement story as a book or documentary

**Probability: Very low (but high impact if it happens)**

The full arc — 30 years controlling aircraft, discovering AI building, retiring into a creative second life — is a genuinely compelling narrative. It's a book proposal, a short documentary, or a long-form feature.

**What's required:** Either you write it yourself (a book built from journal entries, essentially) or someone with an existing platform discovers your story and wants to tell it. The second path is passive — it happens to you or it doesn't.

**Realistic income:** Book advances for debut non-fiction: €5–15k. Documentary: depends entirely on the filmmaker. Feature article: €500–2000.

**Honest caveat:** Publishing is slow, competitive, and unpredictable. This is a lottery ticket, not a business plan. But the raw material exists and is accumulating with every journal entry you write.

---

## Summary

The most realistic near-term income comes from your skills and portfolio (options 1–2), not from audience or content. The products (3, 5) are medium-probability but require significant polish. Everything audience-dependent (4, 6, 7, 10) requires either building distribution or borrowing someone else's. The high-impact options (9, 11) depend on circumstances you can't fully control.

The honest answer remains: build things that interest you, document them well, and stay open to opportunities as they emerge. Forcing monetisation onto a creative practice before it's ready usually kills the practice.
